# The Shape of Attention

What a week of field notes taught us about focus.

---

# We started with a contradiction

Users asked for fewer notifications, then turned every notification back on.

The issue was not volume alone. It was uncertainty:

> “If I do not look now, will I miss something important?”

---

# Four moments in the day

### Morning

Orient me. What changed since yesterday?

### Midday

Focus me. What deserves the next hour?

### Afternoon

Reassure me. Am I still on track?

### Evening

Close the loop. What can wait until tomorrow?

---

# A better mental model

Attention is not a bucket to fill. It is a path to protect.

```text
context -> choice -> focus -> progress -> closure
```

The product should reduce the number of times someone has to rebuild context.

---

# The design response

- Fewer alerts, each with a clear reason
- A daily starting point instead of an endless feed
- Quiet defaults with explicit escalation
- A visible finish line for work in progress

---

# The question we carry forward

What would this feel like if the product respected the user's attention as much as their data?
